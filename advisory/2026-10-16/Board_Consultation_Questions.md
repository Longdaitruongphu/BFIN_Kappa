# Kappa Board Consultation Workbook

16 October 2026 | Companion to the white paper | Discussion questions, not adopted policy

Use one round at a time. For each answer record: preferred decision, reason, acceptable sacrifice, evidence required, authorized decision maker, and review trigger. Do not equate choosing a preference with proving that it is financially feasible.

## Round 1: mandate, identity and risk capacity

1. Is Kappa intended to continue indefinitely after 2030, or is 2030 primarily a review/transfer date? The assignment does not settle its later life. This determines whether to optimize a terminal amount, future spending capacity, or both.
2. Should USD 8m of December-2022 purchasing power remain the reference objective, with nominal forecasts updated for inflation? If a revision is considered, what explicit trade-off justifies it?
3. When you say “growth,” do you mean real wealth after grants, annual positive returns, increased grant capacity, or assets including new donations? Which one will the board measure?
4. Which is more damaging to the mission: temporary market-value losses while payments stay funded, or a high chance/severity of real-wealth shortfall in 2030? State why and give an example.
5. Is 15% a binding trigger for a decision, or an economic floor that must not be breached? If it is a floor, are you willing to reduce the terminal objective or seek additional capital rather than promise incompatible outcomes?
6. How much real terminal shortfall would trustees find manageable: a modest reduction in later discretionary grants, or no reduction at all? Discuss depth and duration, not just a model success percentage.

Suggested first response: answer questions 1-6 before selecting another ETF or a risk-aversion coefficient.

## Round 2: payment protection and real operating needs

7. Do you approve matching both mandatory obligations from inception, including the 2027 payment, rather than protecting only the nearest one?
8. Can protected payment assets ever be replaced? If yes, must replacement cash flows and settlement readiness be verified before the original assets are sold?
9. What actual operating expenses exist: custody, administration, reporting, audit or external management? Which are supplied facts versus optional real-world assumptions?
10. Who pays those costs, on what dates, and are they fixed amounts or proportional to assets? Is there any committed external funding? The current case assumes none.
11. Is a proposed reserve retained capital, expected expenditure, or tactical buying capacity? The same dollars cannot count in all three roles.
12. What operational interruption should the reserve cover, and for how long? Explain the cash schedule that supports the amount instead of choosing a round percentage.
13. Are the early Delta coupons reserved for terminal wealth, approved costs or growth reinvestment? What numerical target adjustment follows any change?
14. May the foundation accept new programme commitments before 2030? If this is a proposed extension, should every new irrevocable commitment be funded before approval?

## Round 3: drawdown, crisis exceptions and decision rights

15. Should the principal drawdown measure be unitized whole-fund NAV, unrestricted assets, the growth sleeve, or several measures with distinct consequences?
16. Is the monitoring frequency daily or month-end? What data and personnel would support it? Monthly evaluation cannot prove a daily stop works.
17. Do the existing 12% warning and 15% escalation levels match the board's intention? What action is compulsory at each level, even if no trade occurs?
18. What observable evidence defines systemic stress? How will the reviewer distinguish a broad-market shock from a strategy-specific failure, valuation error or unauthorized position?
19. Who may approve holding risk after a 15% breach, who independently checks the rationale, and how long can the exception remain active?
20. Which rules remain non-waivable in every crisis? Consider borrowing, protected payments, reliable valuation, disclosure and conflicts of interest.
21. If the fund sells risk after a loss, what evidence and rule permit re-entry? How will the committee assess the risk of locking in losses and missing a recovery?
22. Suppose the fund falls 18%, payment funding remains intact, and markets are disorderly. What additional facts are required before deciding to hold, sell or rebalance?
23. Suppose the fund falls only 8% but a payment is no longer fully funded because of an operational mistake. Which case gets greater priority, and why?
24. After 2027 the illustrative remaining portfolio is approximately 94% equity. Does your 15% philosophy still fit that exposure? What terminal-risk transition would be needed?

## Round 4: inflation and macroeconomic beliefs

25. Which index measures mission purchasing power: broad US CPI-U, another USD index, or an explicit education-cost proxy? How will you explain its basis risk?
26. Should inflation updates change only the nominal target estimate, or also risk limits? Who can make either change?
27. What evidence supports persistent inflation, and what observation would make you reduce confidence in that thesis? Separate supply, demand and expectations channels.
28. What evidence distinguishes near-term AI investment demand from longer-term productivity gains? How will you avoid translating task-level productivity directly into equity returns?
29. What would establish that a market segment is overvalued, beyond rapid price rises or media attention? How much portfolio discretion should that judgment allow?
30. If high public debt coincides with rising long yields and weak growth, does your policy remain feasible? Do not assume that fiscal pressure guarantees monetary easing.
31. Would you prefer a policy robust to several inflation paths over one optimized for your favored macro view? What expected-return sacrifice is acceptable?
32. Which data vintages belong to the 2022 decision, and which belong only to a contemporary learning exercise? Who checks release dates?

## Round 5: growth implementation and benchmarks

33. Is lower volatility worth persistently lower participation in a strong equity market? Express an acceptable trade-off in dollars or future grant capacity.
34. Could trustees remain with the authorized policy after three years of VTI outperformance if mission funding and risk behavior remain consistent with expectations? What evidence would still require a change?
35. If USMV loses its expected risk advantage, what investigation or threshold triggers review? Is it exposure drift, liquidity/costs, index-methodology change, or a sufficiently long change in behavior?
36. Does the A=4 variance penalty reflect your preferences? What concrete scenario choice would reveal that A below 2.58 might fit better? The parameter is not the same as a 15% loss limit.
37. Should a later scenario compare USMV, VTI and a fixed mix under identical funding and cost rules? What objective will rank them before looking at the results?
38. Do you accept a four-layer scorecard: payment delivery, purchasing power, strategic opportunity cost and implementation quality? Which layer can override the others?
39. Should VTI remain the growth opportunity-cost benchmark while the selected ETF's own index measures implementation? How will this prevent benchmark changes from concealing weak decisions?
40. What tracking deviation and underperformance horizon are acceptable? Would you tolerate the observed approximately 8.93% annualized evaluation tracking error versus VTI? This historical estimate is not a future limit guarantee.

## Round 6: dynamic policy, costs and scaling

41. Should the growth policy maintain a constant risk allocation, reduce risk with time, or secure gains when the goal becomes well funded? State the priority if these rules conflict.
42. If projected required return becomes implausibly high, what comes first: new capital, a transparent goal revision, a discretionary-spending adjustment, or increased risk? Existing fixed payments remain protected.
43. If assets exceed the forecast nominal goal in 2029, would you accept lower upside to protect progress? How would you handle inflation surprises after moving to cash?
44. Should rebalancing use a calendar, drift bands or both? What cost, liquidity and payment checks must pass before a trade?
45. What annual operating-cost ceiling is proportionate to USD 12m and the services actually needed? Which additional complexity has a demonstrated net benefit?
46. Do educational values require exclusions or stewardship requirements? If so, which are binding, and how will the group quantify portfolio consequences?
47. What events justify changing a forecast, an investment policy, or the foundation's charter? Who approves each and how are dissent and prior versions preserved?
48. If the foundation grows through donations, what controls and reporting capacity must improve before adding instruments, managers or commitments? Growth in donations is separate from investment performance.

## Recording a board decision

For each approved decision, complete:

| Field | Record |
|---|---|
| Decision ID and effective date | Pending |
| Question and preferred option | Pending |
| Rationale and rejected alternative | Pending |
| Quantified payment/goal/risk/cost impact | Pending |
| Evidence and information cutoff | Pending |
| Proposer and independent checker | Pending |
| Approver, dissent and conflicts | Pending |
| Monitoring metric and next review | Pending |
| Exception expiry or re-entry rule, if applicable | Pending |

Do not fill these fields with invented names, work or approvals. Once the first-round decisions are made, produce a separate numerical scenario and a concise proposed IPS for review. The current frozen original policy remains the baseline for comparison.
