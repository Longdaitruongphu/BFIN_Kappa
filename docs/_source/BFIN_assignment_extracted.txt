The decision 
It is 31 December 2022. Kappa Foundation has received USD 12 million in unrestricted cash. It 
funds education programmes and has made two commitments that cannot be cancelled. There are 
no other assets, liabilities or committed donations. 
Commitment Payment date Amount 
Scholarship programme 31 December 2024 (year 2) USD 2.4 million 
Learning centre 31 December 2027 (year 5) USD 3.6 million 
After meeting these payments, the trustees want the remaining fund at 31 December 2030 to have 
at least the purchasing power of USD 8 million in December 2022. This is a goal, not a promised 
investment return. The two scheduled payments are fixed nominal obligations. 
The trustees are uncomfortable with investment losses approaching 15%, but they have not agreed 
how to measure that concern, over what period, or what compromises they would accept. They 
have not specified an inflation assumption, a return target, a risk-aversion parameter or a policy 
benchmark. 
Eta, the treasurer, proposes using high-quality bonds to support the commitments and putting the 
balance into the portfolio with the strongest historical risk-adjusted performance. Theta, an external 
adviser, argues that factor exposures and persistent abnormal returns should determine the growth 
allocation. Both believe their approach offers a disciplined basis for investment. 
Your group is the independent investment team. Recommend a policy and an initial allocation that 
the trustees could authorize. Decide which assumptions are defensible, what evidence supports 
your recommendation, and which promises the foundation cannot responsibly make. 
The board�s questions 
� What should Kappa own, in what amounts, and under what investment policy? 
� How credible is the plan for meeting the commitments and preserving the longer-term mission? 
� What actually drives the proposed portfolio�s return and risk? Which adviser�s claims, if any, 
survive examination? 
� What evidence would make you change your recommendation? 
There is no prescribed optimal portfolio. A recommendation to change the trustees� goal is 
acceptable if supported by a feasible alternative and a quantified trade-off. You may not change the 
mandatory payments in the original case.
